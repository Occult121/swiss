Lauterbrunnen at a glance This is Lauterbrunnen Lauterbrunnen can be found in a valley of dramatic beauty between Interlaken and the Jungfrau Massif. You’d be hard pressed to find anywhere that feels as much like the Swiss Alps. Discover Lauterbrunnen One way to start your day could be with a stroll through the village, against the backdrop of the Staubbach Falls. Then the best photo spot can be found in the car-free postcard village Wengen. Depending on what takes your fancy at the time, either take the cogwheel railway to Kleine Scheidegg or the cable car to Mürren. The trip up to the Männlichen is worth the ride alone – on the roof of the cable car. Waterfalls in Lauterbrunnen The village gets its name – Lauter Brunnen (loud springs) – from the 72 waterfalls that can be found there. Its landmark is the Staubbachfall: see how it cascades 300 metres over the vertical rock face. The Mürrenbachfall, which plunges 750 meters into the depths in five cascades, is a mighty sight. The Trümmelbachfälle also lets you feel the tremendous energy of the water: the glacier water thunders through a labyrinth of rocks inside the mountain. Wengen and Mürren Enjoy the peace and quiet as well as the views available in the two car-free mountain villages that belong to the municipality of Lauterbrunnen. Wengen is on the sunny side and on the route to Grindelwald. From Mürren the cable car takes you up to Schilthorn with its revolving restaurant Piz Gloria. But before all that, why not take a selfie – with the Eiger, Mönch and Jungfrau in the background? Around Lauterbrunnen Lauterbrunnen is the starting point for the most well-known excursion destinations in the Jungfrau Region. Thanks to the well-developed rail network, you can easily get from one mountain highlight to the next. Tip: take enough time to explore the unique nature around Lauterbrunnen at your own pace – there is so much to experience. Experience Lauterbrunnen for less The Swiss Travel Pass, the Jungfrau Travel Pass and the Bernese Oberland Pass all give you free travel on railways, buses and many cable cars and mountain railways around Lauterbrunnen. Accommodation can be found for less at hostels, youth hostels and campsites. Hundreds of marked hiking paths with panoramic views and barbecue areas offer you a free way of having fun.

GENEVA

Perle du Lac Where Mont Blanc emerges How about a stroll in a wonderful park with views of the lake and the mountains as far as the eye can see? When the weather is clear, Mont Blanc is unveiled, looming mightily on the horizon. Tranquillity and nature await you here, and in all weathers and seasons, you will dream about it with pleasure while sitting on a bench.
St. Peter's Cathedral A panoramic view Historical treasure of the old town, St. Peter’s Cathedral boasts a breathtaking panorama. Let us assure you, the effort to climb the 157 steps that lead to the top of the towers is rewarded with an incredible 360° panorama of the city and the lake.
The Jet d’Eau water fountain was not supposed to be a fountain Back to 1886 when Geneva city opened a hydraulic pumping station on the Rhône River in order to power factory machines. At the end of each day, when the machines were shut down, a valve was used to release the water pressure in the air. People enjoyed it so much that it soon became a remarkable symbol of the city. It was then amplified and relocated to the centre of the Lake to become the Jet d’Eau of today.

The World Wide Web was created in Geneva You’re reading this article on our website, right? So, let’s go back to March 1989 when Tim Berners-Lee, a scientist working at CERN, the European Organization for Nuclear Research, in Geneva, submitted a proposal to his boss. His idea was to develop a radical new way of linking and sharing information over the Internet, and you know what? It led to what we know today as the World Wide Web and was the starting point of website development!
